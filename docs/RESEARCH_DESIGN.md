# Predeclared C-2 research protocol v0.1 — 2026-11-12

## Estimands (three distinct claims)
1. **Historical weather response:** conditional relationship E[Y_t | weather_t, calendar_t, slow-moving capacity/economic covariates]. Out-of-time predictions and weather-normalized contrasts answer weather contributions, NOT anthropogenic climate attribution.
2. **Long-term climate-change contribution:** difference under factual climate distribution versus a scientifically justified counterfactual climate distribution (natural-only ensemble / detection-attribution), holding an explicitly chosen grid/technology/socioeconomic system fixed, integrating weather response and uncertainty. Do NOT infer this from historical trend alone, from C-1 +4°C perturbations or from two observed years.
3. **Future scenario stress:** predicted system response under SSP, GCM, future resource assumptions and PyPSA architecture. Not a probability forecast of real blackouts without verified physical transmission, generator outages, hydro energy/dispatch and operator practices.

## Scope and units
Primary: four BAs CISO, AZPS, BPAT, SRP as a feasibility cohort; report limited geographic coverage instead of calling this entire Western Interconnection. Extend only if source coverage gates pass. Hourly UTC interval-start with explicit BA reporting vintages, energy units and timezone QA. Primary empirical outcome: **EIA-930 reported BA hourly demand** (not conflated with behind-the-meter consumption). Secondary, conditional on available verified observations: wind/solar generation, net load, hydro generation, ramp and demand threshold exceedances. PyPSA outcomes: modeled unserved-load slack MWh, maximum slack MW, stressed-hour count, renewable curtailment, dispatch costs, and shadow prices if stable/identifiable.

## Historical response / attribution A
- Train transparent generalized additive/segmented distributed-lag temperature + humidity (if available) model with temperature thresholds learned on training only; allow season/hour/BA interactions, wind and radiation and heat-wave persistence. Benchmark seasonal naive, degree-day regression and boosted trees. Include lagged outcome only if available at issue time and separately label nowcasting vs weather-conditioned response.
- Controls: hour, weekday, holidays, seasonal trend, COVID/economic structural breaks, annual capacity changes and inter-BA reporting changes; do not control for weather mediators when estimating total weather effect. Explicitly disclose unmeasured confounding and policy/electrification shifts.
- Weather-normalized historical contrasts: compare fitted daily/hourly load under observed weather vs fixed baseline-weather resampling, with **historical weather contribution**, bootstrap by multi-day blocks and by year; report uncertainty and response stability.
- Climate attribution is a separate conditional extension: use CMIP6 historical ALL vs NAT ensembles (if suitable and available), model factual/natural-weather distribution alignment, estimate attributable mean/upper-tail response and interval. Without NAT ensembles and robust detectability, label anthropogenic attribution *not identified*.
- Independent validation: negative controls for implausible weather leads, sensitivity to time trend, spatial exposure weights, alternative train periods, placebo outcomes if appropriate. Do not interpret predictive feature importance as causal climate attribution.

## Future prediction B
- Primary feasible horizon: mid-century 2041–2060 versus reference 1995–2014; scenario SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, preselect 3–5 GCMs on historical performance and independence criteria, no cherry-pick on future result. Distinguish future weather-driven sensitivity with **fixed current-grid resources** from compounded scenario with documented demand/capacity changes.
- **Daily NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 is not hourly.** First evaluate daily peak/energy models; an hourly projection is conditional on independently evaluated hourly weather disaggregation/analog generation, preserving heat-wave and spatial dependencies. Wind, radiation, hydro forecasts require appropriate scenario drivers, and may be withheld if unsupported.
- Freeze predictors, training transforms, model selection and hyperparameter search before held-out years. Candidate temporal splits: train 2020–2022, tune 2023, test 2024–2025 *only if data coverage passes*. Additional rolling-origin validation. No random hourly split, no test-year normalization, no same-event contamination. Quantify MAE, RMSE, mean bias, peak-decile MAE, pinball loss and interval coverage by BA, season and heat-event. Compare all models to strong calendar/weather baselines; report bootstrap uncertainty.
- Downscaled-climate historical ERA5 crosswalk, bias correction on TRAIN/reference periods only, preserve spatial/extreme dependence. Separate GCM variability, model response, calibration, resource portfolio, and PyPSA assumptions via hierarchical uncertainty reporting; avoid a single unjustified combined probability.
- PyPSA coupling: start with constrained BA-level screen and fixed resource assumptions. Validate solver balance, energy-conservation, storage SOC and source-specific capacity; only call outputs assumption-conditioned grid stress. Never claim observed EENS or historical outage probability.

## Bounded experiment matrix and stops
E0 source/clock/coverage gate (no optimization); E1 baseline vs GAM vs boosted trees, held-out evaluation (maximum 3 core models); E2 weather-normalization and 3-way factor deconstruction (heat/wind/solar only where reliable; statistical interactions with uncertainty); E3 two SSPs × 3 GCMs × fixed/current vs one explicitly specified future resource portfolio = 12 future scenario families; E4 PyPSA model screen on 12 representative climate-resource configurations with 2 structural grid variants, maximum 24 core optimizations. Hydro separate only if empirically justified; otherwise sensitivity supplement capped at 8 additional cases.

**Stopping criteria:** E0 if fewer than 3 complete annual cycles, unresolved clock mismatch or irreconcilable BA demand definitions: publish feasibility and stop empirical claims. E1 if test model cannot beat calendar/seasonal naive for preregistered primary metric, restrict to descriptive/benchmark result and do NOT propagate as calibrated predictive projection. E2 historical anthropogenic attribution if no defensible NAT counterfactual: mark unidentifiable and stop that claim. E3 if required CMIP variable not source-validated: drop that outcome, no ad-hoc synthetic scenario. E4 if physical system information missing: stop at screening model, not blackout forecast. No post-hoc unlimited parameter searches.
